01

A household needs more than one status

A family can share an address while its members face very different voter-record situations. A single household-level status hides the difference between a scheduled hearing, an unresolved current record, and a historical record that still needs matching.

VoteReady checks each member independently and turns those situations into a prioritised action plan. The demonstration follows four fictional people across three official assignments, with separate record, notice, and preparation states.

Independent synthetic prototype. VoteReady is not affiliated with the Election Commission of India, does not determine voter eligibility, and does not contact government systems. All records, notices, credentials, and official responses in the demo are fictional or simulated.
02

AI interprets. Rules decide.

The application separates interpretation from decisions. Gemini reads visible clues from a Telugu historical roll and returns bounded English transliterations. Zod validates the structured output before deterministic rules rank possible matches.

Visible roll clues
↓
Gemini interpretation + transliteration
↓
Zod-validated structured output
↓
Deterministic candidate ranking
↓
Citizen reviews and confirms

The fixture produces similarity scores of 82, 79, and 78 out of 100. These are rule-based ranking scores, not identity probabilities or confidence that a person has been found. The citizen must explicitly confirm the candidate.

03

Keep missing information missing

An incomplete notice leaves four important fields unknown: the reason, time, venue, and evidence requirements. The application preserves those unknowns instead of filling them with plausible instructions.

A separately labelled simulated clarification can supply the missing details and three preparation items. Their provenance remains visible, so extracted notice content and simulated follow-up information stay distinguishable.

04

From interface to persistent workflow

Next.js, React, and TypeScript provide the application interface. Supabase PostgreSQL and Prisma persist household and workflow state, while Gemini supplies bounded interpretation behind structured schemas.

The repository documents an end-to-end build with Codex, including the application, validation gates, and deployed workflow checks. Session persistence allows the demonstration to continue after a reload.

07

Next questions

The next evaluation step is a larger synthetic multilingual set, measuring Top-1 and Top-3 matching accuracy, extraction precision and recall, false inferences, and schema validity.

AI request latency is another documented limitation. Repeated p50 and p95 latency and cost measurements would be more useful than treating the README’s single-run observations as a performance guarantee.

08

FAQ

Is VoteReady an official government service?

No. It is an independent synthetic prototype with no ECI affiliation or government-system connection.

Do I need to enter real voter information?

No. The demonstration uses fictional people and records. Its credentials, notices, and official responses are simulated.

Does AI choose a person’s identity or eligibility?

No. AI interprets visible clues, deterministic rules rank candidates, and a citizen explicitly confirms a selection. The prototype does not determine eligibility.

What do the test results measure?

Application behaviour on synthetic fixtures, browser workflows, and deployment checks. They do not measure accuracy against real electoral records.
